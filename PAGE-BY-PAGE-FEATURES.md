# Nura — Page-by-Page Feature List

*Every screen in the app, what it does, and why it's useful. Organized in the order a user actually moves through the app.*

---

## 1. Getting started

### Welcome screen
The first thing anyone sees — app branding, "Log In" / "Create Account." If you're already logged in, it skips straight past this and drops you into the app.
**Useful because:** no friction — returning users never see a login screen again; new users get a clean, single decision.

### Sign Up / Log In
Create an account or sign in (including Google sign-in).
**Useful because:** standard, fast onboarding — nothing to relearn.

### Health Profile Setup
A short form right after signup: cycle length, last period date, and health goals (track period / avoid pregnancy / try to conceive / manage symptoms / general health).
**Useful because:** the app can give *accurate* predictions and *relevant* insights from day one instead of generic advice — it knows why you're here.

### Partner Invite / Join
Where someone enters (or opens a link with) an invite code to connect to a partner's account as a "viewer."
**Useful because:** it's how a partner, without creating their own health data, gets a window into supporting someone else — one code, no complicated setup.

## 2. The main app — 5-tab home base

### 🏠 Home
The daily hub: today's cycle phase, quick "Log period / Log symptoms / Log mood" buttons, a summary card linking to full predictions, personalized care & wellness suggestions, and a shortcut to book a doctor.
**Useful because:** it's a one-glance morning check-in — you don't have to dig through menus to log today or see what phase you're in.

### 📅 Tracker
The full calendar: past and future days color-coded by cycle phase, with markers for logged flow/symptoms. Tapping any day opens the **day-log sheet** — a colorful, icon-based form covering menstrual flow, ovulation symptoms, sex & sex drive, mood (15 options), physical symptoms, vaginal discharge, digestion, pregnancy tests, exercise, sleep, water, weight, temperature, stress, medication, and notes. Also holds Cycle Settings (cycle length, period length, last period date, reminders, Pregnancy Mode).
**Useful because:** this is the actual data-entry engine of the app — the more detailed and painless logging is, the more accurate every prediction and insight downstream becomes. Most trackers make you dig through five separate screens for this; here it's one tap, one sheet.

### 🤖 AI Chat (Nura AI)
Tap the center avatar and (after a one-time mic permission) land in a live chat — type or talk — with Nura AI. It answers questions about periods, discharge, cramps, hygiene, intimacy, and body changes in plain language, and explicitly tells you when something needs a real doctor instead of just an app answer.
**Useful because:** 2am "is this normal?" questions get an immediate, judgment-free, medically-aware answer instead of a scary Google search.

### 👥 Community
An anonymous-friendly forum, browsable by topic (PCOS, Fitness, Skin, and more), where people post, comment, like, and support each other in real time.
**Useful because:** periods and women's health are still under-discussed — a built-in, moderated space to ask and share removes the awkwardness of asking elsewhere.

### 👤 Profile
Account settings, notification & privacy toggles, and the doorway to every secondary feature below: Documents, Graphs & Reports, Government Schemes, App Lock, Partner Sync, and more.
**Useful because:** it's the control center — one place to manage the account and reach everything that isn't in the daily tab bar.

## 3. Understanding your cycle (deeper than a calendar)

### Cycle Predictions
A dedicated screen with a circular countdown to your next period, your fertile window and ovulation date, a 5-month cycle-length trend chart, and a plain-language confidence read on how regular your cycle actually is.
**Useful because:** instead of a single guessed date, you get the *range*, the *trend*, and *how much to trust it* — which is what actually matters for planning.

### Symptom Predictions
Shows the likelihood of each symptom (cramps, headaches, mood changes, etc.) by cycle phase — calculated from *your own* logged history, not a generic chart.
**Useful because:** knowing "I usually get headaches 2 days before my period" lets you actually prepare instead of being surprised every month.

### Daily Insights
A short daily explanation of what's happening hormonally today, plus tailored exercise, nutrition, sleep, and wellness suggestions for your current phase.
**Useful because:** it turns "cycle science" into one relevant sentence and three doable actions for *today*, not a wall of theory.

### Graphs & Reports
A visual dashboard: colorful stat cards for cycle length, weight, basal body temperature, water intake, and sleep, plus a Reports section linking to the Symptom Report and the downloadable doctor PDF.
**Useful because:** trends are easier to *see* than to remember — this is where you'd notice "my sleep has been dropping for a month" before it becomes a bigger problem.

### Symptom Report
A ranked breakdown of your most logged symptoms, moods, and flow days over the last 90 days, plus your sleep/water/stress averages.
**Useful because:** it's a fast "what has this cycle actually been like" review — useful for you, and as prep before a doctor visit.

### Symptom Checker
A guided questionnaire that screens for possible signs of PCOS, endometriosis, fibroids, or thyroid issues, and keeps a history of past check-ins.
**Useful because:** these conditions are commonly under-diagnosed for years — an early, low-friction nudge toward "this pattern is worth mentioning to a doctor" can genuinely shorten that gap.

## 4. Medical records & AI on your own data

### Documents
A secure vault for prescriptions, lab reports, bills, and scans — upload, filter by type, sort by date. Includes an AI-powered document summarizer and generates the downloadable "doctor report" PDF (your cycle + symptom history, AI-analyzed, in a chosen language).
**Useful because:** your health paperwork is scattered across pharmacies, labs, and clinics in real life — this keeps it in one place, and turns raw pages into something a doctor can actually use in five minutes.

### Document Chat
Ask questions about your own uploaded documents in a chat interface, with answers optionally read aloud.
**Useful because:** most people can't fully parse a lab report — this lets you ask "what does this number mean?" directly, instead of waiting for your next appointment.

## 5. Habits & gamified wellness

### Wellness Forest
Your consistency literally grows a tree — log daily care (habits, water, symptom logs, reflection) and it visibly grows and blooms; skip days and it gently droops. The scenery (sky, hills, weather) shifts mood with your current cycle phase, and past cycles are kept as a browsable history of trees.
**Useful because:** habit streaks as plain numbers are easy to ignore; a living thing that responds to your care is a much stronger, kinder motivator.

### Habits
Suggested simple routines (sleep 8 hours, eat iron-rich food, stretch) that you check off daily, earning reward points and growing your own progress visual (Resting Seed → Blooming Tree).
**Useful because:** it breaks "be healthier" into three concrete, low-effort daily actions instead of a vague goal.

## 6. Real care, beyond the app

### Doctor Consultations
Browse real clinical specialists, see their live availability, book a paid session, video-call them directly in the app, chat, and receive a prescription.
**Useful because:** this is the point where "an app" becomes "actual healthcare" — no separate booking site, no waiting room.

### Period Buddy
Get matched with a peer for chat and video support — a companion going through similar cycle experiences.
**Useful because:** some things are easier to talk about with someone anonymous but relatable than with a doctor or even a friend.

### Blogs
Categorized, easy-to-read educational articles on periods, health, and wellness.
**Useful because:** a trustworthy, in-app reading list beats an open-ended web search for health topics.

### Reels
Short-form educational videos on the same topics.
**Useful because:** some people learn faster from a 30-second video than a paragraph — this meets them there.

## 7. Education hubs

### Hygiene Guide
Practical, categorized advice: choosing and changing period products, daily intimate hygiene, and staying comfortable while swimming, bathing, or exercising.
**Useful because:** this is exactly the kind of practical detail that's often skipped in sex-ed, answered here without embarrassment.

### Sexual Wellbeing
Covers intimacy during periods, STI prevention, a contraception comparison, and consent & safety.
**Useful because:** puts sensitive, important topics in one respectful, judgment-free place instead of scattered, inconsistent sources.

### Health Issues Hub
Dedicated guidance sections for PCOS/PCOD, endometriosis, fibroids, pregnancy wellness, irregular cycles, and menopause.
**Useful because:** these are the conditions that actually drive most period-related worry — having them addressed directly (not just "see a doctor") builds real understanding.

## 8. Government Schemes & Benefits *(a feature almost no period app has)*

### Government Schemes & Benefits
Browse real Indian government health schemes — national and Rajasthan state — related to women's reproductive and menstrual health. Filter by category, search, and see recommended schemes.
**Useful because:** many people never learn these benefits exist. Surfacing them inside a health app, right when they're relevant, turns awareness into access.

### Scheme Details
Full scheme information, an honest eligibility checker ("you may be eligible" — never a false guarantee), and the right next action per scheme: apply, check eligibility, find the nearest government facility (via Google Maps), or learn more.
**Useful because:** the *right* next step differs per scheme — this avoids sending everyone to a generic "apply now" that doesn't apply to them.

### My Applications
Tracks the schemes you've applied to or checked.
**Useful because:** it's easy to lose track of multi-step government processes — this keeps a simple record in one place.

## 9. The partner's side of the app

A connected partner gets their own mirrored 5-tab experience — separate from the data owner's, and built entirely around **what the owner has chosen to share.**

### Partner Sync (owner side)
Where the data owner invites a partner with a code and — critically — controls exactly what's shared, switch by switch: cycle phase, intimacy notes, alerts, and reports are each independently opt-in.
**Useful because:** "sharing your period app with your partner" usually means all-or-nothing. Here, nothing is visible until you explicitly turn it on.

### Partner Home (Overview)
A read-only dashboard for the partner: shared cycle phase, and **smart care suggestions** that translate raw data into actual guidance — e.g. "she's feeling anxious today — a calm evening and no big decisions would help" — plus do's/don'ts and streak visibility.
**Useful because:** most partners don't know what a "luteal phase" means or what to *do* with that information — this hands them a direct, actionable answer instead of raw data to interpret themselves.

### Partner Chat
Direct messaging between the data owner and their connected partner.
**Useful because:** keeps cycle-related conversations in the same app the context lives in, instead of switching to texts.

### Partner AI Voice Assistant
The same voice-enabled Nura AI, available to the partner too.
**Useful because:** a partner can get educated on their own, in the moment, without having to ask potentially awkward questions directly.

### Partner Community
The exact same community forum the owner sees.
**Useful because:** partners can learn from other people's experiences too, not just their own partner's.

### Partner Profile
The partner's own account settings: notifications, app lock, disconnect, logout.
**Useful because:** the partner has their own identity in the app, not just a guest view.

## 10. Settings & trust

### Notifications
A central feed for reminders, partner activity, and cycle updates — the same screen for both the data owner and a connected partner.
**Useful because:** one place to catch up, instead of a scattered mix of push notifications.

### App Lock
PIN or biometric lock on top of the app itself.
**Useful because:** health and intimacy data is sensitive — this keeps it private even if someone else picks up the phone.

---

## Why this matters as a whole

Laid out page by page, the pattern is clear: **every screen either makes tracking easier, makes the data mean something, or connects it to real help** — a real doctor, a real partner, a real government benefit, or a real community. Most period apps stop at the calendar. Nura keeps going until the data actually *does* something for you.
